## Oscar Fidel Antunez Martinez is a pediatric nurse with expertise in parenting and childhood obesity. He earned his Bachelor of Science in Nursing (BSN) at the Universidad Nacional Autónoma de Honduras (UNAH) and obtained his Master of Science in Nursing (MSN) from the Federal University of Rio Grande (FURG), Brazil, and his international PhD in Nursing at the National Taipei University of Nursing and Health Sciences (NTUNHS), Taiwan.
His primary research focus lies in pediatric nursing, particularly the prevention of childhood obesity through family-centered and culturally sensitive approaches. He has investigated how parental practices, health education, and social determinants of health influence children’s behaviors and long-term health outcomes. This line of work reflects his broader commitment to improving child health equity in Latin America.
In addition to pediatrics, his research extends to nursing leadership and advanced practice roles in emergency and critical care. His studies have explored how nurses in resource-limited systems, such as Honduras, experience leadership responsibilities, confront institutional barriers, and envision the potential for advanced practice nursing (APN) and clinical nurse specialist (CNS) roles in strengthening healthcare delivery.
Oscar also has a strong interest in nursing education and research competencies. His work highlights the need to address the deficiency of nursing publications in Honduras and Latin America by fostering stronger research skills among nursing faculty and providing institutional support for scholarly activity. By identifying barriers to research productivity, he aims to contribute to a stronger culture of evidence-based nursing education in the region.
Methodologically, he integrates quantitative and qualitative approaches—including cross-sectional studies, psychometric validation, in-depth interviews, triangulations, and grounded theory—to generate robust evidence that addresses the complexities of nursing practice. He has contributed to the translation, cultural adaptation, and validation of instruments that assess nursing knowledge and attitudes in critical care and emergency settings, ensuring their reliability and relevance in diverse contexts.
Through his academic and professional journey across Honduras, Brazil, Japan, Indonesia, and Taiwan, Oscar Fidel Antunez Martinez has developed a truly international perspective on nursing science. His work bridges clinical practice, education, and research, with a consistent focus on improving care in resource-limited healthcare systems. He is committed to strengthening the role of nurses as leaders, educators, and researchers, while promoting international collaboration to advance nursing knowledge and practice globally.
